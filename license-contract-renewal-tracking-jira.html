Tracking software license and contract renewals in Jira

Licenses and vendor contracts fail differently from certificates. Nothing breaks on the day. Instead the contract renews itself for another year at the old seat count, or the notice period passes while everyone assumed someone else was looking. This guide covers how to keep renewal dates in Jira so the right person hears about them in time.

What to record for each renewal

  • The date that matters. For a contract with a notice period this is the last day you can cancel, not the end date.
  • An owner. The person who decides whether to renew, not the person who pays the invoice.
  • Vendor and cost. With these in the list, the reminder issue already contains what the owner needs to decide.
  • A note. Seat count, contract number, where the PDF lives.

Do you need Jira Service Management Assets for this?

Assets can model licenses and contracts as objects with date attributes, and automation rules can act on those dates. It is the right tool when you already run a full asset inventory. It is also only available on Premium and Enterprise plans, and it takes real work to design the schema and the rules.

If all you need is "tell the owner before the date", there are lighter ways.

Option 1: one issue per renewal with a due date

Create a project or an issue type called "Renewal", put the date in the due date field, assign it, and build a board or a filter sorted by due date. A filter subscription can e-mail the list of upcoming renewals every week.

What goes wrong: after the renewal the issue is closed and the next year's issue is never created. A second problem is that a weekly list is easy to ignore; nobody is told "this one is yours and it is 30 days away".

Option 2: Jira automation on due dates

A scheduled automation rule can search for issues whose due date is within 30 days and comment, transition or notify. Cloning the issue with a new date on close solves the "next year" problem.

This works well if someone on the team is comfortable maintaining automation rules. Expect to spend time on the edge cases: several reminder stages, not reminding twice, and what happens when a date is edited.

Option 3: Expiry Tracker

Expiry Tracker is a Jira app with one list for licenses, contracts, domains and SSL certificates. It works on Jira Standard and does not need Assets.

  1. Add an item: type License or Contract, a name, the date, an owner, and optionally vendor, cost and a note.
  2. A Jira administrator chooses the project and issue type for reminders.
  3. Once a day the app checks every date. By default it creates an issue 60, 30 and 7 days ahead, assigned to the owner, with the vendor, cost and note in the description. The thresholds can be changed.

When you renew, edit the date. The reminders start again for the new date, so there is no "next year" issue to remember. The "Next 90 days" view shows what is coming up, and the whole list can be exported as CSV for finance.

What it does not do: it is not a contract repository or a license compliance tool. It does not store documents or count installed seats. It tracks dates and makes sure someone is told.

Recurring work without an expiry date

Some obligations have no end date but must happen on a schedule: a quarterly backup restore test, a yearly access review, a monthly patch window. Expiry Tracker has a "Recurring check" item type for these. You enter the next due date and an interval, and the app creates one issue per due date and moves the check forward by itself.
