Domain expiry monitoring in Jira

Registrars send renewal notices, and domains still expire. The notices go to the address of whoever registered the domain years ago, the card on file has been replaced, or auto-renew was switched off during a transfer. When a domain lapses, the website, the e-mail and every certificate on it stop at the same time. This guide shows how to get the warning into Jira, where it is assigned to a person instead of sent to a mailbox.

Where the expiry date comes from

Every domain has a public registration record. The modern way to read it is RDAP, the successor to WHOIS, which returns structured data over HTTPS. For example, for a .com domain:

curl -s https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/example.com | grep -o '"eventAction":"expiration"[^}]*'

Not every registry offers this. Some country-code registries, for example .de, .io and .eu, do not publish an expiry date in machine-readable form. For those the date has to come from your registrar's control panel.

Option 1: a list and a calendar

List your domains with their expiry dates and put the dates into a shared calendar or into Jira due dates. This is enough for three domains with one registrar.

What goes wrong: the list is not updated after a renewal or a transfer, and nobody notices that auto-renew failed, because the list still shows last year's assumption.

Option 2: a script

Run the RDAP query above from cron for each domain, compare the date with today, and create a Jira issue through an automation webhook when it gets close. You will also need to find the right RDAP server for each top-level domain (IANA publishes the list), handle registries that answer differently, and avoid creating the same issue every day.

Option 3: Expiry Tracker

Expiry Tracker is a Jira app that keeps the domain list in Jira and reads the dates for you.

  1. Add an item of type Domain with the domain name and an owner.
  2. The app looks up the expiry date at the domain's registry when you save, and again once a day. If a domain is renewed, the date in the list follows by itself.
  3. By default the owner gets a Jira issue 60, 30 and 7 days before expiry, in the project an administrator chose.

If the registry does not publish the date, the item says so and you enter the date by hand. If a lookup fails, the reason is shown next to the item instead of the item silently keeping an old date.

The only thing the app sends outside Atlassian is the domain name itself, to the registry responsible for it.

What it does not do: it does not renew domains or talk to your registrar. It watches the public record and tells the owner.

Why this is worth doing even with auto-renew

Auto-renew fails quietly: an expired card, a registrar account nobody can log in to, a domain that was moved and lost the setting. A reminder that reads the registry's own record does not depend on any of these. If the date has not moved 30 days before expiry, something is wrong with the renewal, and someone has a Jira issue saying so.

The same list can hold your SSL certificates and your licenses and contracts. Setup details are in the documentation.
